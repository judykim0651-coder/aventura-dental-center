## What Is a Dental Crown?

**Content Wireframe:**

- Definition: A dental crown is a custom-made, tooth-shaped cap that fits over a damaged or weakened tooth to restore its shape, size, strength, and appearance.
- Analogy: "Think of it as a helmet for your tooth — protecting what's left while restoring full function."
- Explain full-coverage vs. partial coverage (onlay/inlay).
- When crowns are used: root canal protection, fractured teeth, large fillings, cosmetic enhancement, bridge abutment, implant restoration.
- **Visual suggestion:** Cross-section diagram of a tooth with a crown in place (placeholder: 500×350).

> **Key Point:** Crowns are custom-fabricated in a dental lab to match the exact shade, contour, and occlusion of your natural teeth.

## Signs You May Need a Crown

**Content Wireframe:**

Symptom checklist with explanatory paragraphs:

- **Large or failing filling** — When a filling exceeds 50% of the tooth's structure, a crown provides better protection.
- **Fractured or cracked tooth** — Hairline cracks that don't extend below the gumline are crown-repairable.
- **After root canal therapy** — Endodontically treated teeth become brittle; a crown prevents fracture.
- **Severe discoloration** — Internal staining (tetracycline, trauma) that bleaching can't fix.
- **Worn-down teeth** — Bruxism, acid erosion, or age-related wear.
- **Dental bridge abutment** — The supporting teeth for a bridge require crowns.
- **Cosmetic improvement** — Misshapen, malformed, or poorly proportioned teeth.

- **Callout box:** "**Wondering if you need a crown?** Look for these red flags: tooth pain when biting, visible cracks, sensitivity to hot/cold that lingers, or a filling that feels rough or loose."

## Types of Crown Materials

**Content Wireframe:**

Comparison table:

| Material | Strength | Aesthetics | Cost Range | Best For |
|----------|----------|------------|------------|----------|
| **Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM)** | Very high | Good | $1,000–$2,500 | Molars, high bite force areas |
| **Lithium Disilicate (e.max)** | High | Excellent | $1,200–$2,800 | Anterior teeth, single crowns |
| **Zirconia (Monolithic)** | Extremely high | Very good | $1,100–$2,500 | Molars, bruxers, bridges |
| **Porcelain (Layered)** | Moderate | Excellent | $1,000–$2,400 | Front teeth, cosmetic priority |
| **Gold Alloy** | Extremely high | Poor (metal) | $1,200–$2,700 | Molars, high-wear patients |
| **Composite Resin** | Moderate | Moderate | $800–$1,800 | Temporary crowns, budget option |

- Detailed paragraph on each material:
  - **PFM:** Time-tested, strong core with natural-looking porcelain fused on top. Risk of dark gum line over time as gums recede.
  - **e.max:** Single-block lithium disilicate — superior translucency and strength. Our top recommendation for most patients.
  - **Zirconia:** The strongest material available. Tooth-colored but more opaque; excellent for high-stress areas.
  - **Gold:** The gold standard (pun intended) for longevity — least wear on opposing teeth, best marginal fit.
- Recommendation framework by tooth position and patient profile.

## The Crown Procedure

**Content Wireframe:**

Step-by-step walkthrough:

### First Appointment (Prep & Temporization)
1. Numbing and tooth preparation (reduction of 1.5–2mm all around)
2. Impression or intraoral scan (ITERO digital scan — no gagging, no goop)
3. Shade selection using a shade guide
4. Placement of a temporary crown (resin, cemented with temporary cement)
5. Lab sends final crown in 1–2 weeks (or same-day with CEREC)

### Second Appointment (Delivery)
1. Removal of temporary crown
2. Try-in: checking fit, contact points, occlusion
3. Adjustments if needed
4. Cementation (permanent adhesive)
5. Final occlusion check and polish

- **Visual suggestion:** Side-by-side illustration showing a prepared tooth vs. crowned tooth (placeholder 600×300).

## Crown Care and Longevity

**Content Wireframe:**

### How Long Do Crowns Last?
- Average lifespan: 10–15 years (with proper care)
- Can last 20+ years with excellent hygiene and regular checkups
- Factors that shorten lifespan: bruxism, poor hygiene, gum disease, trauma

### Care Instructions
- Brush twice daily with a non-abrasive toothpaste
- Floss daily — use a floss threader or superfloss around the crown margin
- Avoid chewing ice, hard candy, or opening packaging with your teeth
- Nightguard if you grind or clench

### When to Replace a Crown
- Recurrent decay at the margin (most common failure mode)
- Chipped or fractured porcelain
- Open margin due to cement washout
- Gum recession exposing the margin

- **Warning callout:** "Crowns don't last forever. Regular 6-month checkups let us spot early signs of trouble before the tooth underneath is compromised."

## Cost and Insurance

**Content Wireframe:**

### Typical Cost Range
- Single crown: $1,000–$3,000 (depends on material, location, lab fee)
- CEREC same-day crown: $1,200–$2,800
- Premium all-ceramic (e.max/zirconia): $1,500–$3,000

### Insurance Coverage
- Most dental insurance covers 50% of crown cost (major restorative category)
- Annual maximum typically $1,500–$2,000 per person
- Some plans have a waiting period for major procedures

### Payment Options at Aventura Dental Center

## FAQs

**Content Wireframe:**

1. **Does getting a crown hurt?**
   - Answer paragraph (local anesthesia makes it painless; mild sensitivity after is normal)

2. **Can a crown be whitened?**
   - Answer paragraph (porcelain doesn't respond to bleach; choose shade before fabrication)

3. **What happens if a crown falls off?**
   - Answer paragraph (keep it, call us — may be recemented or need replacement)

4. **How long does the crown procedure take?**
   - Answer paragraph (two visits ~45–60 min each, or single visit with CEREC)

5. **Can I get a crown on an implant?**
   - Answer paragraph (yes — implant crowns are screw-retained or cement-retained)

6. **What's the difference between a crown and a veneer?**
   - Answer paragraph (crown covers entire tooth; veneer covers only the front surface)

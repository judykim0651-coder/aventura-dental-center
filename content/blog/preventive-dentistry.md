## What Is Preventive Dentistry?

**Content Wireframe:**

- Opening definition: Preventive dentistry is the practice of caring for your teeth to keep them healthy and avoid cavities, gum disease, enamel wear, and more.
- Core philosophy: "An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure" — nowhere is this truer than in dentistry.
- The preventive model vs. the reactive model: Instead of waiting for pain or visible problems, preventive dentistry involves regular professional care and daily habits that stop disease before it starts.
- Brief mention of the patient-dentist partnership: you handle the daily routine, we handle the professional oversight and early detection.

> "Preventive dentistry isn't just about avoiding cavities. It's about preserving your quality of life, avoiding pain, and saving thousands in restorative treatment down the road."

## The Three Tiers of Prevention

**Content Wireframe:**

### Primary Prevention — Avoiding Disease Altogether
- Fluoride treatments, dental sealants, dietary counseling, oral hygiene instruction.
- Target: patients with no current disease.
- Goal: keep healthy patients healthy.

### Secondary Prevention — Early Detection and Intervention
- Regular exams, X-rays, periodontal probing, oral cancer screenings.
- Target: early-stage disease (incipient caries, early gingivitis).
- Goal: catch problems when they're small, reversible, and inexpensive.

### Tertiary Prevention — Halting Progression of Established Disease
- Fillings, root canals, periodontal therapy, crowns.
- Target: active disease already present.
- Goal: stop further damage and restore function.

## Essential Preventive Services at Aventura Dental Center

**Content Wireframe:**

### Comprehensive Oral Exams
- Detailed head, neck, and oral cavity examination.
- Periodontal charting (pocket depths, recession, bleeding points).
- TMJ screening and bite analysis.
- Oral cancer screening (visual and tactile — standard of care at every exam).

### Professional Cleanings (Prophylaxis)
- Supragingival and subgingival scaling to remove calculus.
- Polishing to remove stain and smooth tooth surfaces.
- Fluoride varnish application for added protection.

### Digital Radiographs (X-Rays)
- Bitewing X-rays for interproximal cavity detection (annually).
- Panoramic X-ray for overall assessment (every 3–5 years).
- CBCT (3D imaging) for implant planning, impacted teeth, pathology.
- **Digital = 80–90% less radiation than traditional film.**

### Dental Sealants
- Thin plastic coating applied to the chewing surfaces of molars.
- 80% effective at preventing cavities in sealed teeth for the first 2 years.
- Recommended for children, teens, and adults with deep grooves.

### Fluoride Treatments
- Professionally applied varnish (2.26% sodium fluoride).
- Recommended every 6–12 months for moderate-to-high caries risk patients.
- Also available: prescription fluoride toothpaste for at-home use.

### Oral Cancer Screenings
- Visual inspection of all soft tissues: tongue, cheeks, palate, floor of mouth, lips.
- Palpation of lymph nodes and salivary glands.
- Adjunctive screening tools: VELscope or similar fluorescence technology.
- **1 in 60 men and 1 in 141 women will develop oral cancer in their lifetime — early detection saves lives.**

## Preventive Care by Age Group

**Content Wireframe:**

### Children (0–12)
- First dental visit by age 1 or within 6 months of first tooth.
- Fluoride varnish applications starting at tooth eruption.
- Sealants on first permanent molars (age 6–7) and second molars (age 12).
- Habit counseling (thumb sucking, pacifier use).
- Orthodontic screening by age 7.

### Teens (13–19)
- Continued 6-month recall schedule.
- Orthodontic evaluation and treatment (braces or clear aligners).
- Wisdom tooth monitoring.
- Sports mouthguard fabrication.
- Dietary counseling: sports drinks, energy drinks, and their erosive effects.

### Adults (20–64)
- Risk-based recall intervals (every 3–12 months depending on risk).
- Periodontal maintenance for gum disease history.
- Cosmetic preventive care (whitening maintenance, veneer care).
- Nightguard fabrication for bruxism (teeth grinding — affects 10–15% of adults).

### Seniors (65+)
- Root caries prevention (receding gums expose root surfaces).
- Dry mouth management (medication-induced xerostomia affects 40%+ of seniors).
- Implant and denture maintenance.
- Oral cancer screening at every visit.
- Regular review of medications affecting oral health.

## The Financial Argument: Prevention Costs Pennies Compared to Restoration

**Content Wireframe:**

### The Cost Comparison Table

| Preventive Service | Typical Cost | Restorative Alternative | Typical Cost |
|-------------------|-------------|------------------------|-------------|
| Routine cleaning + exam | $200–$400/year | Single filling | $150–$400 |
| Fluoride treatment | $25–$50 | Root canal + crown | $2,000–$4,000 |
| Sealant (per tooth) | $30–$60 | Implant + crown | $4,000–$6,000 |
| Periodontal maintenance | $100–$200/visit | Full-mouth reconstruction | $20,000–$50,000+ |

- **Return on investment:** Every $1 spent on prevention saves $8–$50 in restorative care.
- **Insurance alignment:** Most dental plans cover 100% of preventive care — making it essentially free for insured patients.
- **The hidden cost of delay:** Small cavity → large filling → root canal → extraction → implant. Each step is exponentially more expensive.

> "A $50 fluoride treatment today can prevent a $4,000 implant tomorrow. The math is simple — the mouth just doesn't send bills until it's too late."

### Real-World Scenario
- **Patient A:** Visits every 6 months, has insurance, pays $0–$200 out of pocket per year for preventive care. Lifetime dental cost (age 20–80): ~$6,000–$12,000.
- **Patient B:** Avoids the dentist for 10 years, then needs: 5 fillings ($1,500), 1 root canal + crown ($3,000), 2 extractions + implants ($10,000). Total: ~$14,500 — and that's a conservative estimate.

## Your At-Home Prevention Toolkit

**Content Wireframe:**

Daily essentials the dentist recommends:

1. **Soft-bristle toothbrush** (electric preferred — Oral-B or Sonicare)
2. **Fluoride toothpaste** (1,000–1,500 ppm fluoride — non-negotiable)
3. **Dental floss** (waxed or unwaxed — whichever you'll use consistently)
4. **Tongue scraper** (reduces bad breath bacteria by 75%)
5. **Fluoride mouthwash** (use at a different time than brushing)
6. **Sugar-free gum** (xylitol-sweetened — stimulates saliva after meals)

### Optional but Recommended
- **Water flosser** (Waterpik) — especially for implants, bridges, braces
- **Nightguard** — if you grind or clench at night
- **Prescription fluoride toothpaste** — for high-risk patients

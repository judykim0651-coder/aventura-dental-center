## What Are Clear Aligners?

**Content Wireframe:**

- Definition: Clear aligners are a series of custom-made, removable thermoplastic trays that gradually move teeth into position.
- Evolution of orthodontic treatment: metal braces (1880s) → ceramic braces (1980s) → clear aligners (1999–present).
- How they work: each aligner applies controlled force to specific teeth, moving them 0.2–0.3mm per stage. Wear each tray 20–22 hours/day for 1–2 weeks before progressing to the next.
- Brands discussed: Invisalign, ClearCorrect, SureSmile, 3M Clarity, and in-house aligner options.
- **Visual suggestion:** Progression strip showing aligner stages 1, 10, 20, 30 with simulated tooth movement (placeholder: horizontal 6-panel, 800×200).

> "Clear aligners have revolutionized orthodontics — not by being better than braces, but by removing the psychological barrier to treatment for millions of adults."

## Clear Aligners vs. Traditional Braces

**Content Wireframe:**

| Factor | Clear Aligners | Traditional Braces |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Appearance** | Nearly invisible | Visible metal brackets and wires |
| **Removable** | ✅ Yes (eat, brush, floss normally) | ❌ Fixed for entire treatment |
| **Comfort** | Smooth plastic, minimal irritation | Wires and brackets can abrade cheeks/lips |
| **Diet** | No restrictions (remove to eat) | Avoid sticky, hard, crunchy foods |
| **Oral Hygiene** | Normal brushing and flossing | Requires special floss threaders and proxies |
| **Treatment Duration** | 6–18 months (mild to moderate cases) | 12–36 months (all complexity levels) |
| **Complexity** | Mild to moderate crowding, spacing, bite issues | All cases including severe malocclusion |
| **Compliance Required** | High (must wear 20–22 hrs/day) | None (fixed appliance) |
| **Emergency Visits** | Rare (can revert to previous tray if lost) | Broken brackets, poking wires |
| **Cost** | $3,000–$7,000 | $3,000–$8,000 |

- Sub-section: "When braces are still the better choice" — severe rotations, large anteroposterior corrections, skeletal discrepancies.
- **Callout box:** "**Aventura Insight:** Most of our adult patients with mild-to-moderate crowding are excellent clear aligner candidates. We'll do a free smile assessment to determine yours."

## The Clear Aligner Process

**Content Wireframe:**

### Step 1: Consultation & Digital Scan
- Clinical exam, photos, and digital impressions (ITERO scan).
- Discussion of goals, expectations, and treatment timeline.
- CBCT if needed for skeletal/airway assessment.

### Step 2: Digital Treatment Plan
- ClinCheck or proprietary software simulates tooth movement in 3D.
- Provider adjusts staging, attachments, interproximal reduction (IPR) points.
- You preview the final result before aligners are fabricated.

### Step 3: Aligner Fabrication
- Lab produces all aligners (20–40+ trays depending on case).
- First set delivered in 2–4 weeks.

### Step 4: Active Treatment
- Wear each tray for 20–22 hours/day.
- Change trays every 7–14 days as prescribed.
- Progress checks every 6–10 weeks.
- Attachments (small tooth-colored bumps) may be bonded for grip.

### Step 5: Retention
- After active treatment, wear a retainer full-time for 3–6 months, then nightly.
- Without retention, teeth WILL relapse — this is not optional.

## Who Is a Good Candidate?

**Content Wireframe:**

### Ideal Candidate Profile
- Mild to moderate crowding or spacing
- Minor bite issues (overjet, overbite, crossbite)
- All permanent teeth present and healthy
- Good oral hygiene habits
- No untreated gum disease or active decay
- Compliance: willing to wear aligners 20–22 hrs/day

### Less Ideal Candidates
- Severe crowding or rotations > 20 degrees
- Large skeletal discrepancies (may need surgery + orthodontics)
- Active periodontal disease
- Heavy bruxism (aligners will be destroyed)
- Patients who can't commit to wear time

### Age Considerations
- Teens: good option, but compliance is a concern — compliance indicators built into aligners.
- Adults: the primary demographic — median age ~32.
- Seniors: never too late; aligners can improve function and hygiene access.

- **Callout box:** "Think you're not a candidate? Schedule a consultation anyway — digital simulations give us a clear picture of what's possible."

## Cost and Insurance Coverage

**Content Wireframe:**

### Typical Cost
- **Full treatment (both arches):** $3,500–$7,000
- **Single arch:** $2,000–$4,000
- **Express/smile-only (minor front teeth):** $1,500–$3,500
- **Retainers only:** $300–$600 per set

### Insurance
- Orthodontic benefits: many plans cover 50% of treatment up to a lifetime max ($1,500–$2,500)
- FSA/HSA: treatment is eligible for pre-tax dollars
- **Payment options:**
  - In-house payment plans (0% interest for 12 months)
  - CareCredit / Sunbit financing
  - Family discounts when multiple members treat

## Daily Life with Aligners

**Content Wireframe:**

### Eating and Drinking
- Remove aligners before consuming anything except plain water.
- Recommended wear: 20–22 hours/day → allows 2–4 hours of total out-of-mouth time.
- Meal timing strategies: three 30-min meals + one snack break.

### Oral Hygiene Routine
1. Remove aligners before brushing.
2. Brush teeth thoroughly.
3. Floss (easy since teeth never have wires blocking access).
4. Brush aligners (cool water, soft brush — no toothpaste, it's abrasive!).
5. Soak aligners in cleaning crystals or ultrasonic cleaner daily.

### What to Expect
- **First week:** lisp, mild pressure/discomfort, increased salivation — all normal.
- **Tray change days:** 1–2 days of mild soreness, then zero discomfort until next tray.
- **Attachments:** can feel slightly rough initially; smooth out within days.

- **Pitfall alert:** "The biggest mistake new aligner patients make is not wearing them enough. Missing just 4 hours/day doubles treatment time."

## Results and Retention

**Content Wireframe:**

### What to Expect
- Minor changes visible within 4–6 weeks.
- Final result viewable in ClinCheck simulation before starting.
- Typical treatment time: 6–18 months.

### Retention — The Most Important Part
- Teeth move back (relapse) without retention — guaranteed.
- Immediate post-treatment: wear retainer 24/7 for 3 months.
- After 3 months: switch to nighttime wear only.
- Replace retainer every 1–3 years (it wears out).
- **Visual suggestion:** Before/after comparison grid — 4 patient cases with different malocclusion types (placeholder: 2×2 grid, 600×600).

> "Alignment is the journey. Retention is the destination. Don't skip the retainer — or you'll do it all over again."

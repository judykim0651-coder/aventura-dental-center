What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants in Aventura, FL are a long-lasting way to replace missing teeth with a stable foundation placed in the jaw. An implant is a small titanium post that acts as an artificial tooth root, surgically positioned into the jawbone. Over time, the bone fuses to the implant through a process called osseointegration, creating a secure anchor.

Once healed, the implant can support a custom crown (single tooth), a bridge (multiple teeth), or a full arch restoration. Unlike dentures or bridges, implants replace both the root and the visible portion of the tooth, helping preserve bone structure and maintain facial contours.

What to Expect — Dental Implant Procedure

  1. Comprehensive exam and digital imaging to evaluate bone density and plan implant placement.
  2. Implant post placement surgery — the titanium post is surgically inserted into the jawbone.
  3. Healing period (osseointegration) — 3–6 months for bone to fuse around the implant.
  4. Abutment placement and custom crown attachment — the final restoration is secured to the implant.
  5. Follow-up visits to monitor healing and ensure long-term success.

How to Care for Your Dental Implants

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristle toothbrush, especially around the implant crown and gum line.
  • Floss daily using unwaxed tape or implant-specific floss.
  • Rinse with an alcohol-free antimicrobial mouthwash.
  • Avoid chewing hard objects (ice, hard candy) on the implant crown.
  • Visit your dentist for regular checkups and professional cleanings.
  • Wear a nightguard if you grind your teeth to protect the implant.

Am I a Candidate for Dental Implants?

  • Missing one or more teeth — implants replace individual teeth or multiple teeth at once, with no impact on healthy adjacent teeth.
  • Healthy gums — active gum disease should be treated first; once your gums are stable, implants can typically proceed.
  • Adequate jawbone — sufficient bone density is needed to anchor the titanium post; patients with some bone loss may be candidates after discussing bone grafting options at a consultation.
  • Non-smoker or willing to quit — smoking can interfere with healing and is a factor Dr. Bistritz will review with you.
  • Committed to home care — implants thrive with consistent brushing, flossing, and routine professional cleanings.

The best way to confirm candidacy is a personal consultation with Dr. Bistritz, who evaluates bone structure, gum health, and overall health before recommending a treatment plan.

The Dental Implant Process, Step by Step

  1. Consultation and 3D imaging — Dr. Bistritz reviews your dental and medical history, evaluates bone density with digital imaging, and discusses the full treatment plan before any procedure begins.
  2. Preparatory care (if needed) — any active decay, gum disease, or insufficient bone is addressed first. If bone grafting is recommended, your dentist will explain the timeline before implant placement.
  3. Implant post placement — the titanium post is surgically placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia. Most patients return to light activity within a day or two.
  4. Osseointegration healing window — the bone gradually fuses to the implant over a period that typically ranges from 3 to 6 months, creating a stable anchor for the final restoration.
  5. Abutment and custom crown — once healing is confirmed, an abutment connects the implant to a custom crown that is color-matched and shaped to blend naturally with your smile.
  6. Follow-up visits — periodic checkups allow Dr. Bistritz to confirm the implant is integrating properly and that surrounding gum tissue remains healthy.

Timelines are described as typical ranges because each patient heals at their own pace. Your actual schedule will be outlined at your consultation.

Recovery and Aftercare

In the days immediately following implant placement, mild soreness and some swelling around the surgical site are normal. Over-the-counter pain relief and cold compresses as directed by Dr. Bistritz are typically sufficient. Most patients are able to return to routine daily activities quickly, though individual recovery varies.

  • Soft foods first — stick to soft, easy-to-chew foods in the days after placement to avoid putting pressure on the healing site.
  • Gentle oral hygiene — continue brushing the rest of your mouth normally; follow your dentist's instructions for cleaning around the implant site while it heals.
  • Avoid hard objects — once healed, chewing ice, hard candies, or non-food objects can stress the crown; treat your implant crown as you would a natural tooth.
  • Long-term maintenance — brush twice daily, floss using implant-appropriate floss or a water flosser, and keep your scheduled professional cleanings. Routine checkups allow Dr. Bistritz to monitor the implant and surrounding bone over time.
FAQ

Dental Implant Questions

With proper care and regular dental visits, dental implants can last many years and often decades. Longevity depends on factors such as your oral hygiene habits, bone health, and whether you attend routine checkups and cleanings. Dr. Bistritz will advise you on the best maintenance routine for your individual situation.

The implant placement procedure is performed with local anesthesia, so you should not feel sharp pain during the surgery. Some soreness and mild swelling in the days after placement is normal and typically manageable with over-the-counter care as directed by Dr. Bistritz. Every patient heals differently, so your dentist will guide you through what to expect.

Age alone is rarely a barrier for dental implants. The main considerations are adequate jawbone density, healthy gums, and overall health. Dr. Bistritz evaluates each patient individually at a consultation to determine whether implants are appropriate, so the best way to find out is to schedule an exam.

Dental implant crowns are custom-made and color-matched to blend with your surrounding teeth. Because the crown is shaped like a natural tooth and anchored below the gum line, most people cannot tell the difference. Your dentist will work with you to select the right shape and shade.

Care for a dental implant much like you would a natural tooth: brush twice daily with a soft-bristle toothbrush, floss daily using implant-specific floss or unwaxed tape, rinse with an alcohol-free antimicrobial mouthwash, and attend regular professional cleanings and checkups. Avoiding chewing on hard objects and wearing a nightguard if recommended also helps protect the restoration long term.

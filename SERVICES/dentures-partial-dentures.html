What Are Dentures and Partial Dentures?

Dentures are custom-made removable appliances that replace missing teeth and surrounding tissue. Full dentures replace an entire arch (upper or lower) when all teeth are missing. Partial dentures fill in gaps when some natural teeth remain, attaching to adjacent teeth with precision clasps or connectors for a secure fit.

Modern dentures are crafted from high-quality acrylic and sometimes reinforced with metal or flexible materials for added durability and comfort. They restore your ability to chew a wider variety of foods, speak clearly, and smile with confidence — improving both function and quality of life.

What to Expect — Denture Process

  1. Comprehensive exam and consultation to assess oral health and discuss tooth replacement options.
  2. Digital or traditional impressions taken for precise fit.
  3. Try-in appointment — wax model fitted to check appearance and bite alignment before final fabrication.
  4. Final dentures delivered and adjusted for comfort.
  5. Follow-up visits to fine-tune fit as gums and bone heal and change shape over time.

How to Care for Your Dentures

  • Remove and rinse dentures after eating to remove food debris.
  • Brush dentures daily with a soft denture brush and non-abrasive denture cleaner.
  • Soak overnight in denture solution or water to keep them moist and prevent warping.
  • Handle with care — hold over a towel or water basin if dropped.
  • Clean your gums, tongue, and any remaining teeth daily.
  • Visit your dentist for annual checkups to evaluate fit and reline if needed.
  • Replace dentures every 5–7 years or as your dentist recommends.

Types of Dentures We Offer

The right type of denture depends on how many teeth are missing, the health of any remaining teeth, and your goals for comfort and stability. Dr. Bistritz reviews each option with you at your visit.

  • Full (complete) dentures — replace all of the teeth in an upper or lower arch when no natural teeth remain, resting on the gums to restore your bite and your smile.
  • Partial dentures — fill in one or more gaps when some healthy natural teeth remain, using discreet clasps or connectors that also help keep the surrounding teeth from shifting.
  • Implant-supported dentures and overdentures — snap onto or are held by dental implants for added stability and a more secure bite when you want less movement than a traditional removable denture.

Is a Denture Right for You?

A denture may be a good fit if you:

  • Are missing several teeth, or all of the teeth in an upper or lower arch
  • Prefer a removable, non-surgical way to replace missing teeth
  • Have an older denture that has become loose, worn, or no longer fits comfortably
  • Want to restore chewing, speech, and facial support while keeping to a comfortable budget

Dentures are not the only choice. Some patients are better suited to a fixed option such as a dental bridge or an implant-based restoration. Every mouth is different, so Dr. David S. Bistritz evaluates each case at a consultation in our Aventura, FL office and recommends the approach that best fits your needs.

The Denture Process, Step by Step

  1. Consultation and impressions — Dr. Bistritz examines your gums and any remaining teeth, discusses your options, and takes digital or traditional impressions so your denture can be built to fit.
  2. Custom fabrication — your denture is crafted to your measurements, and you help choose a tooth shade and shape that look natural for your smile and facial features.
  3. Fitting and adjustments — the finished denture is placed, your bite is checked, and small adjustments are made so it feels as comfortable and secure as possible.
  4. Follow-up — as your gums and bone settle over time, follow-up visits let Dr. Bistritz fine-tune the fit and, when needed, reline the denture.

If you would like a more secure result, ask whether pairing your denture with dental implants is an option for you.

Adjusting to and Caring for Your Dentures

A short adjustment period is normal with new dentures. For the first few weeks many patients notice more saliva, minor soreness, or that eating and speaking take some practice — this typically eases as your mouth adapts. Start with softer foods, cut them into smaller pieces, and if a sore spot lingers, a quick adjustment at our office usually resolves it.

  • Clean daily — brush your dentures with a soft denture brush and a non-abrasive cleaner, and rinse them after meals.
  • Remove at night if advised — many patients take their dentures out to sleep and soak them, giving the gums a chance to rest.
  • Handle carefully — clean them over a folded towel or a basin of water so they are protected if dropped.
  • Keep regular checkups — routine visits let Dr. Bistritz check the fit, reline when needed, and keep your gums and any remaining teeth healthy.
